Confluence Data Center is ending: what are your options?

Atlassian is retiring its self-managed Data Center products. Here is the full timeline, what each date means for your Confluence, and how to decide between Cloud, staying put and a self-hosted alternative.

Jérémie ConstantBuilds Turtle at novalutions. Updated 27 November 2026.

Short answer

Confluence Data Center reaches end of life on 28 March 2029. After that date your instance becomes read-only and receives no more security fixes. New customers could no longer buy it from 30 March 2026, and existing customers can buy expansions only until 30 March 2028.

You have three realistic options. Move to Confluence Cloud, keep Data Center running until 2029 and decide later, or move to a self-hosted wiki you control. We build Turtle, one of those self-hosted options, so read our list with that in mind. We name its gaps next to its strengths.

What end of life means for Confluence Data Center

On 28 March 2029 your Data Center subscription and its Marketplace apps expire. Atlassian says the environment then becomes read-only. Your pages stay readable, but nobody can edit them in that instance.

Until that date Atlassian still provides technical support, fixes for critical security vulnerabilities and the connectors to Atlassian Cloud. After it, there are no product updates, no support and no new security fixes.

Some products are exempt. Bitbucket Data Center and Bamboo Data Center do not reach end of life, and existing customers get a Bitbucket Hybrid License. Jira Align Data Center is also excluded. Confluence, Jira, Jira Service Management and Crowd Data Center are all included.

Atlassian also mentions exceptions for some customers: extended maintenance after 2029 and multi-year Data Center extensions. Both are "by exception only". Atlassian does not publish criteria, so do not plan around them unless you have one in writing.

What happened to Confluence Server

Confluence Server went first. Support for all Atlassian Server products ended on 15 February 2024. Since then there are no bug fixes, no security patches and no renewals. If you still run Confluence Server, you have been without security fixes for almost three years. The decision you face is the same as for Data Center, only more urgent.

Your three options

Option 1: Move to Confluence Cloud

This is the path Atlassian built the timeline around. You keep Confluence, your users keep the interface they know, and Atlassian runs the servers. Atlassian offers migration help, including a programme called FastShift for migrations above 1,000 seats.

If you cannot use shared cloud infrastructure, Atlassian offers Isolated Cloud. It is a single-tenant environment that Atlassian hosts and manages, and it is now generally available. For US government customers there is Atlassian Government Cloud.

What you give up: your data moves to infrastructure you do not operate. Cloud pricing is per user and recurring. Not every Data Center app has a Cloud version, so check each Marketplace app you depend on. For regulated or data-sovereignty-sensitive teams, this option needs a legal and security review first.

Option 2: Stay on Data Center until the end

You can renew for your current users and apps until 28 March 2029. This buys time and is a reasonable choice if you have a large instance, many apps and no budget for a migration this year.

What you give up: from 30 March 2028 you cannot add seats or buy new apps. If your team grows, you are stuck. Every month you wait also shortens the window for a calm migration. Plan the move while you still have support, not after the instance turns read-only.

Option 3: Move to a self-hosted alternative

If the reason you ran Data Center was control over your data, a self-hosted wiki keeps that. You choose the server, the location and the backup policy. With an open-source licence, no vendor can end the product's life for you.

What you give up: Confluence-specific features. Macros, Marketplace apps and some page layouts have no direct equivalent anywhere else. You also take on running the software yourself, or pay someone to run it. Expect to rebuild some content by hand.

Self-hosted Confluence Data Center alternatives

A short list of open-source wikis that run on your own server. For a longer comparison, see our guide to open-source Confluence alternatives.

Turtle

Licence: AGPL-3.0, no paid edition

Turtle is a self-hosted wiki where people and AI agents write documentation together. It ships as one binary with SQLite, so there is no database server to run. Every page reads and writes as Markdown, and a built-in MCP server lets AI clients work within each member's permissions. Turtle can import spaces from Confluence Data Center and Confluence Cloud. Its gaps are real: no page-level permissions, groups, SSO, comments or templates yet. It is early-stage software, and the data format can change before 1.0.

Docmost

Licence: AGPL-3.0 core, separate Enterprise licence for enterprise code

Docmost is a collaborative wiki with spaces, real-time editing, comments, page history and diagrams. It has a Confluence importer that takes a Confluence HTML space export. On Docmost's pricing page, the importer is listed under the paid Business plan, together with SSO, page-level permissions and AI. Audit logs and SCIM sit in Enterprise. Check which features your team needs before you assume the free Community edition covers them.

BookStack

Licence: MIT

BookStack organises content into books, chapters and pages. It runs on PHP and Laravel with MySQL, and supports OIDC, SAML2 and LDAP logins plus TOTP-based MFA in the free version. It has no paid edition. BookStack does not list a built-in Confluence importer, so plan for an export and conversion step. Its book structure also differs from Confluence spaces, which means some reorganisation.

XWiki

Licence: LGPL

XWiki is a mature, extensible wiki with a large set of community extensions. It is closest to Confluence in flexibility. Its Confluence Migrator Pro imports from Confluence XML backups, including users, permissions and page history. The migrator is a paid product, sold as part of the Confluence Migration Toolkit. Other "Pro Applications" are also paid. XWiki is heavier to operate than the other three.

Migration checklist

Work through this list while your Data Center instance is still supported.

  1. Inventory your spaces. List every space, its owner and when it was last edited. Archive what nobody uses instead of migrating it.
  2. List your Marketplace apps and macros. Note which pages depend on them. These are the parts that will not move cleanly to any target.
  3. Map permissions. Write down who can see and edit each space. Check whether your target supports the same model, for example page-level restrictions or groups.
  4. Decide on authentication. If you need SSO, confirm the target supports it in the edition you plan to use.
  5. Run a test import. Export one representative space and import it into the target. Compare pages, attachments, links and tables side by side.
  6. Fix and repeat. Note what broke, decide what to rebuild by hand, and repeat with a second space.
  7. Plan the cut-over. Freeze edits in Confluence, run the final import, redirect links and tell your users.
  8. Keep a read-only copy. Keep the old instance or a full export until you are sure nothing is missing.

For Turtle, the step-by-step version is in how to migrate from Confluence. Turtle reads the CSV or XML export with attachments. The tree, attachments, page links, labels and mentions come across, some macros only approximately, and every import leaves an Import report and can be undone.

How to choose

Choose Confluence Cloud if your organisation accepts a hosted service and depends on Atlassian apps or Jira integration. It is the lowest-effort path for your users.

Choose to stay until 2029 if a migration this year is not possible. Set a decision date well before 30 March 2028.

Choose a self-hosted alternative if data control is the reason you ran Data Center. Then pick by what you need today. If you need SSO, groups and page-level permissions now, Turtle does not have them yet. If you want a simple install, Markdown and AI agent access, Turtle fits.

Frequently asked

When is Confluence Data Center end of life?

On 28 March 2029 at 23:59 PST. After that, Confluence Data Center becomes read-only and receives no more support or security fixes.

Can I still buy Confluence Data Center?

Not as a new customer. Sales to new customers ended on 30 March 2026. Existing customers can buy new subscriptions and expansions until 30 March 2028 and renew until 28 March 2029.

Will my Confluence data be deleted in 2029?

Atlassian says the instance becomes read-only, so your content stays accessible. You cannot edit it, and the software no longer receives security fixes. Running unpatched software on your network is a risk, so plan to move the content.

Is Bitbucket Data Center affected?

No. Bitbucket and Bamboo Data Center are excluded from the end of life. Jira Align Data Center is excluded too.

What is the best Confluence Server alternative?

Confluence Server lost support in February 2024, so the same options apply. Pick based on your requirements: Cloud for continuity, or a self-hosted wiki such as Turtle, Docmost, BookStack or XWiki for control.

Does Turtle import from Confluence?

Yes. Turtle imports Confluence spaces from Data Center and Confluence Cloud, and you can try it in the demo.
